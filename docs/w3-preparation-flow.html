COUGAR · Porto Alegre · Workshop 3

From a chosen site to a project someone can actually say yes to

W2 ends with an organization that has a place, a hazard in their own words, and a family of solutions they are curious about. W3 turns that into a specific intervention, sized and costed — and, just as importantly, into the list of what they still don't know: what to investigate, who to call, what to measure on the ground, and what to write down.

Working document, in English. The flow itself ships in pt-BR — every phrase quoted here is a gloss of Portuguese copy, not the copy.

01 — WHAT W3 STARTS WITH

W3 does not ask a single question W2 already answered

These fields arrive filled. They are not just context — each one is an input to a rule that generates dossier items later. If one is empty, the dossier gets thinner in a specific, nameable way.

bairro · site polygonWhere. Drives which municipal bodies are even relevant, and the area in m².
current_useVegetated / paved / abandoned / mixed. Decides whether demolition, de-paving or soil recovery is on the list.
land_tenureOwn it / formal agreement / city's but we use it / no guaranteed access. The single strongest predictor of the contact list.
site_worryAlagamento · Inundação · Enxurrada · heat · slope. Their word, not the raster. Decides which evidence is worth collecting.
site_knowledge_depththin / partial / strong. A thin read means W3 opens by filling gaps, not by choosing.
photos + documentsWhat they already shared. Anything already photographed is not asked for twice.
Teia Sprint applicationpurpose: teia_sprint. A semi-formal proposal already exists — W3 refines it rather than starting blank.
prior_collaborationYes/no + who, in their words. Shapes whether a partner is someone they already know.
nbs_interestFamílias they were drawn to. Pre-filters (never excludes) the selector.
role_preferenceConsulted / write / manage funds / implement / coordinate. Decides who owns each dossier item.
02 — THE WORKSHOP, BEAT BY BEAT

Six stages, one session

~75 minutes with a facilitator in the room. The left column of each card is what the organization experiences; the right is what the platform captures and what that answer generates. Colour tells you which of the four lists an answer feeds.

Investigate People to contact Gather & measure Document
~5 MIN · NO NEW DATA

Ground the room in what they already said

The site, the hazard in their own words, and their photos come back on screen. "Is this still the place?" If anything moved, it moves here — not silently later.

In the room

  • Their site on the map, their photos, their word for the hazard
  • One confirm chip, or a correction

Captured

site_confirmed_w3site changes, if any
3a · ~20 MIN · open_intervention_selector

Choose the solution — ordered by their mechanism, nothing hidden

The 27-solution catalogue, ordered so the answers to their water reach the top: a river that overtops surfaces margin and várzea recovery before rain gardens. Every solution stays reachable — the flow promises "nada fica descartado" and that stays literally true. Real cases are one tap away from the question itself.

In the room

  • Browse, compare, open examples without losing their place
  • Pick 1–2 solutions (a combination is normal)
  • "Why this one, here?" — one or two sentences, in their words

Captured & generated

intervention_types[]justification_why_here combination_rationale
  • The solution's ficha names who must approve it — that becomes the seed of the contact list
  • Every technical prerequisite the ficha names ("needs an infiltration test", "needs a geotechnical study") becomes an investigation item
  • Cost band per m² is now known, so a budget becomes a writable document
3a · ~20 MIN · SKETCH + CHIPS

Size it, and say who builds it

They drag a polygon over their own site — area in m² is computed, not asked. Then the build model: mutirão, hired contractor, university/NGO partnership, mixed, undecided. This pair is what turns "a rain garden" into something with a number and a crew.

In the room

  • Drag the corners of a footprint on the map
  • See where their size lands: small / medium / large, with a cost range
  • Tap the build model

Captured & generated

intervention_area_geojsonintervention_area_m2 intervention_scale_bandconstruction_model
  • construction_model × land_tenure resolves the whole permission question — see the matrix below
  • Area × cost band → a first budget figure to verify against a real quote
  • A footprint crossing a slope, a sidewalk or a drainage line raises its own investigation item
3b · ~15 MIN · IMPACT & BASELINE

What is it like today, and what would change

The agent brings the evidence base — comparable cases with ranges and a stated confidence, never a single invented number. What it needs back is the baseline: how the place is now, in their description, because nothing can be shown to have improved without it.

In the room

  • "How is the place today, before anything?" — free text
  • Timeframe: 6 months / 1 year / 2 years / phased
  • Who could measure: us / a university partner / nobody yet

Captured & generated

baseline_conditionexpected_impact project_timeframemonitoring_capacity
  • Baseline evidence to collect before anything is built — the mechanism decides what: a high-water mark for inundação, the water's path downhill for enxurrada, shade and surface temperature for heat
  • "Nobody can measure" is not a dead end — it is a request for a monitoring partner, named as such
  • A one-page baseline record, dated and photographed
3c · ~15 MIN · OPERATIONS & MONEY

Who keeps it alive after the mutirão goes home

The failure mode for every one of these solutions is not construction — it is year two. The fichas say it plainly: a clogged rain garden becomes a permanent puddle and, after three or four days, a mosquito site. So maintenance is asked about as a named responsibility, not a hope.

In the room

  • Who maintains it: us / volunteers / city partnership / contractor / TBD
  • How often, and roughly what a year costs
  • Where recurring money would come from

Captured & generated

who_maintainsmaintenance_frequency opex_estimate_year1sustainability_model
  • A maintenance agreement — with the city if the land is theirs, internally if it is not
  • "TBD" on maintenance names a conversation the coordination team has to broker
  • Whether the chosen funding line actually covers maintenance, or only construction
~10 MIN · THE OUTPUT

The dossier reads itself back, and gets owners

Nothing here is new input. Every item was generated by a rule from an answer they already gave, so the last ten minutes are spent on the only two questions that matter: who does this, and by when — with role_preference proposing the owner and the org overriding it.

In the room

  • Four short lists, read out loud
  • An owner per item, a date per item
  • "This one isn't us" → it moves to the coordination team's list, visibly

Leaves with

  • A shareable plan: what it is, how big, what it costs, who says yes, who maintains it
  • A homework list they can work through between workshops
  • Named people and bodies to approach, not "the city"
  • A field checklist for the next visit to the site
03 — HOW THE LISTS ARE GENERATED

Four lists, and the rules behind them

The dossier is derived, not authored. Every item traces to a rule over data the repo already holds — the per-solution fichas already say who must approve each of the 27 solutions and what technical study it needs. That is the difference between a checklist an agent improvised and one a coordinator can audit.

Investigate

Technical unknowns that decide whether the design survives contact with the ground.

Solution → its named prerequisite A rain garden needs a soil infiltration test, or it won't drain. A rain terrace needs a geotechnical study before the wall material is chosen. These are stated per solution already. shared/nbs-solution-fichas.ts · quemPrecisaDizerSim
Mechanism → the physical question Enxurrada asks where the water enters and how fast. Inundação asks how high it got. Alagamento asks where it pools and how long it stays. shared/site-knowledge.ts · WORRY_SUBTYPES
current_use → site preparation Paved means de-paving and a compaction check. Abandoned means finding out what was there before, and whether the soil is contaminated. E2_CURRENT_USE
Funding line → what it actually covers Most community lines fund construction and not maintenance. Confirming that before applying is cheaper than discovering it in year two. knowledge/_financing-sources/cbo-grants.md

People to contact

Named bodies and roles, with what each one is being asked for.

Who has to say yes Porto Alegre routing is already written per solution: SMAMUS for the urban environment, DMAE for anything touching public drainage, EPTC when the work touches roadway or sidewalk, Defesa Civil where slope stability is in play, FEPAM at state scale. shared/nbs-solution-fichas.ts · flagged autorizacaoEstimada where routing is our inference
Landowner, when it isn't them Tenure of public-informal or public-no-access puts a permission conversation ahead of everything else — before a budget, before a mutirão date. E2_TENURE × the matrix below
The technician the design needs A mutirão can dig and plant. It cannot calculate flow or slope. The dossier names which half needs a professional, so the org isn't told a flat "you need an engineer". ficha · "mutirão pode fazer … o projeto precisa de técnico"
People they already know If they named partners in W2, those names come back as the first place to look — rather than the platform proposing strangers. prior_collaboration_detail · partnerships

Gather & measure

What to bring back from the next visit to the site. Short, physical, doable with a phone.

Baseline before anything is built Dated photographs from fixed points, so "it used to flood here" becomes something a funder can see. Nothing can be shown to have improved without this. 3b · baseline_condition
Measurements the design needs Where the water enters and leaves, the drop across the site, the distance to the nearest public drain, roof and paved area draining into it. solution ficha × intervention_area_m2
Who benefits, counted Households on the affected stretch. "About twelve families on Rua X" is the difference between a level-1 and a level-3 problem statement in the maturity scoring. problem_clarity rubric
Existing paper Any land-use permission, association statute, previous project report or the Teia Sprint application itself — collected as documents rather than described from memory. documents · purpose

Document

What they need to have written down before anyone can fund or authorise this.

The project sheet What it is, where, how big, what it costs, who builds it, who maintains it. Assembled from what W3 captured — not a form they fill again. generated from sections 3a/3b/3c
The permission letter or use agreement Only appears when tenure says it is needed, addressed to the body the routing rule named, stating the footprint and the duration. land_tenure × delivery
Budget and maintenance plan Construction from area × cost band, then a separate annual figure — kept separate precisely because funders treat them differently. costBand · opex_estimate_year1
Terms of responsibility for their own data Ana's drafted document, reaching the org inside the flow rather than as a PDF a coordinator holds. Raised by the orgs themselves at the convening. backlog #31 · CboDataNoticeDialog
04 — THE RULE THAT DOES THE MOST WORK

Access × build model → who has to say yes

The convening found orgs already running gardens and composting, unsure which interventions they can do alone and which need the municipality. The useful answer is not "you need the prefeitura" — it is which half they need it for. Permission and construction are different conversations, with different people, on different timelines.

Provisional. The delivery classification across the 27 solutions is flagged in the catalogue as our read, not verified against the source manuals; the same is true of the agency routing in the fichas. The matrix is honest about being a first draft — Robson and Hesioni should confirm both before it is presented as a verdict to an organization.

05 — WHAT IT LOOKS LIKE FOR ONE ORGANIZATION

A worked example, end to end

Using the test-fixture organization, so nothing here is a real org's data. Everything below the divider was generated by the rules above from the inputs above it — no item was written by hand.

Horta Raízes · Sarandi

Community garden on a city-owned lot they have used for six years. The street pools after every heavy rain and takes two days to clear.

site_worry: alagamento land_tenure: public-informal current_use: mixed intervention: jardins-de-chuva delivery: mutirao area: 320 m² construction_model: mutirão who_maintains: our organization role_preference: executar
Contact Ask SMAMUS for written permission to build on the lot The land is the city's. They can build a rain garden themselves — the obstacle is permission, not capability, and this is the longest-lead item in the whole plan. org · 2 weeks
Contact Confirm with DMAE whether the garden may connect to the public drain Only needed if the overflow ties into the network. If it infiltrates entirely on site, this item disappears — worth establishing before drafting anything. org · 2 weeks
Contact Find a technician for the layer design and infiltration calculation The mutirão can dig and plant. A badly calculated garden does not drain, and the failure looks identical to the problem they are trying to fix. coordination · 3 weeks
Investigate Soil infiltration test at the proposed footprint Named directly in the rain-garden ficha. Decides the depth of the stone layer and whether 320 m² is the right size at all. technician · 4 weeks
Investigate What is under the paved half of the lot current_use: mixed — de-paving cost and compaction are unknown until someone lifts a corner and looks. org · next visit
Investigate Whether the Teia Sprint line funds maintenance or only construction They plan to maintain it themselves, which is credible for trash and replanting but not for restoring filter layers at year 5–10. org · 3 weeks
Gather Photograph where the water pools, and how long it stays Alagamento is defined by pooling and drainage time. Two dated photos — during rain and 48h later — are the baseline the whole impact claim rests on. org · next heavy rain
Gather Measure the paved area draining into the site, and the distance to the nearest drain Both feed the sizing. A phone and a tape measure are enough. org · next visit
Gather Count the households on the stretch that floods Turns "it floods here" into "it reaches about twelve families on Rua X" — the step between a generic and a specific problem statement. org · 2 weeks
Document The permission request itself — footprint, duration, what is being built Drafted from what W3 already captured; the org edits rather than composes. org + coordination
Document Budget at R$ 400–700/m² for 320 m², separated into build and annual upkeep Kept as two figures because funders treat them differently, and because the second is the one that decides whether this exists in three years. org · 4 weeks
Document Who clears the trash, and how often The named failure mode: a clogged garden becomes standing water, and standing water becomes a mosquito site within days. A name and a frequency, not an intention. org · at W4
06 — WHAT EXISTS AND WHAT DOESN'T

Honest build state

The flow above is the design. Roughly half of it is already in the repo; the dossier itself is not.

In the repo

The permission knowledge

All 27 solutions carry who-must-approve and who-maintains-after, with Porto Alegre agencies named and inferences flagged. This is the hardest content in the whole dossier and it already exists.

In the repo

Selector, mechanism ordering, delivery chips

The intervention selector, the hazard-mechanism ordering, and the mutirão/parceria/licença classification all shipped. W3's first beat has its machinery.

Not built

The dossier generator

The rules in section 03 do not exist as code. This is the substantive new piece — deterministic, testable, and independent of the model.

Not built

Sizing bands and the sketch tool

The footprint sketcher and the small/medium/large bands with cost ranges per solution are specified but unwritten. Area currently comes from the W2 pin, not a drawn polygon.

Not built

encontro-3.md

Phase 3 still runs off the monolithic prompt rather than a dedicated skill file, unlike E1 and E2.

Blocking for launch

The intervention detail pages are English-only

Six detail documents, no Portuguese versions — so an org reading a solution in depth gets English body text under Portuguese tabs. This is the largest volume of English a CBO will ever encounter, and W3 is exactly where they meet it.

Needs a person

Confirmation of the routing

Delivery classification and agency routing are our reads. Robson and Hesioni should confirm before any of it is presented to an organization as what they must do.

Needs a person

The need → partner map

Beyond the municipal bodies, naming which partner to approach for funding, technical design or monitoring needs authoring with people who know the local ecosystem.
