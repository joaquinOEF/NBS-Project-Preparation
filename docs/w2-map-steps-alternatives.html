NBS Project Builder · COUGAR · Workshop 2 · map steps

The map is the hardest part of Workshop 2. Three ways to make it simpler and harder to break.

Everything else in W2 is a chip tap. The two map moments are the only place where a community organization has to hit a target, wait for tiles, and interpret a legend — and they sit at the very start, before any of the value shows up. This sets out what the steps are today, what actually goes wrong, and three different ways to fix it.

The evidence that this is genuinely hard. Driving these steps from an automated test at an iPhone viewport, a single tap on the centre of the map failed often enough that the harness needed a retry loop walking outwards through eight different offsets before a bairro would select. Retrying the same point never helped — it is a targeting problem, not a timing one. The desktop test never sees it.

If a scripted harness that knows exactly where to tap needs eight tries, a person holding a phone will tap, see nothing happen, and tap again somewhere else.

Worse, there is a recorded case where the bairros are not merely hard to hit but invisible: with one zone source the fill opacity computes to zero, so the polygons render blank and the screen reads as "no clickable zones, just an overlay" (MAP-ZONE-INVISIBLE, UX backlog). Nothing on screen tells the user the map is waiting for a tap.

Three alternatives

They differ in how much they trust the map as an input device. Mockups in English; the shipped UI is pt-BR.

AOne action per screen Keep the map exactly where it is, but never ask for two things at once. Each screen has one affordance and one primary button.
What changes
The tour becomes one screen with the three hazards side by side and a single "Entendi", not three sequential screens. The zone step loses the legend, the chips and the second button — leaving a map, a title that says "toque no seu bairro", and nothing else. The site step asks "como prefere marcar?" first and then shows only the mode chosen.
Why it might be enough
Most of the observed failures are competition for attention rather than the map itself. Halving what is on screen at each moment is the cheapest intervention that touches every one of them.
What it does not fix
Hitting a polygon is still hitting a polygon. If the fill renders blank, the user still faces an empty screen with no instruction — this makes that failure clearer, not impossible.
Effort
Small–medium, entirely in MapMicroapp. No change to the flow or the data.

Fixes that apply whichever we choose

None of these are alternatives to each other — they are the failsafe layer, and most are small.

  • Never render a step with no visible target. If zone fill computes to zero or the layer is empty, say so and offer the list instead of showing a blank map.
  • Acknowledge a missed tap. Today nothing happens at all; a brief "toque em cima do bairro" after a tap that hits nothing would close the loop.
  • Grow the tap target — a tap within a tolerance of a polygon should select the nearest one rather than requiring a hit inside it.
  • Stop shipping the whole city to the phone. ⚠️ Not a caching problem — see the measurement below. The four reference layers already serve from committed snapshots in 1–10 ms; what blocks is 1.6 MB of JSON downloaded and then filtered to one bairro in the browser with ray-casting. Filter server-side by the bairro's bounds and the overlay mostly stops being necessary. Keep the corner chip and timeout regardless.
  • Drop sticky tooltips on touch and close on pointer-out.
  • Touch targets to ≥ 36 px across map chrome; the 10 px remove control is unusable with a thumb.
  • Re-bake the hazard ramps so green never means danger on one layer and safety on the next — this is data-pipeline work, but the tour shows all three back to back.
  • Translate the loading and tooltip strings that are still hard-coded English.

Decided design (/refine, 31 July)

Not one of the three wholesale — the tour survives, because its purpose is sound; what was missing is the thing that makes the purpose reachable.

DecisionWhy
Keep the one-hazard-per-screen cycleSide-by-side is impossible at 390 px, and the map is a separate tab where the chat is not visible — the cycle is the right shape for that constraint, not a compromise.
Draw their bairro's outline on every hazard screenThe tour exists so an organization can see how their territory compares to the rest of the city. Today the raster covers the whole metro region with nothing marking them, so the comparison is intended but undeliverable. The outline is what turns three pictures into a comparison.
One check after all three"Isso bate com o que vocês vivem aí?" — bate / mais ou menos / não bate. One decision rather than three, taken while the data is actually on screen. A "não bate" is a disagreement signal before we have asked anything else.

Warn about the inverted ramp in words nowrampWarning() already detects it. Say plainly on the flood screen that green is the higher risk here; re-baking all three onto one scale is September pipeline work.

The challenge I owe you, since you asked. You said you weren't sure how relevant the tour is. I think the intent is right and the execution currently cannot deliver it — a city-wide raster with no indication of where you are teaches nothing you can act on, which is probably why it feels uncertain. Outlining their bairro is the cheapest change that makes it do what it was always for. And the check afterwards may end up more valuable than the tour itself: "não bate" tells us our data misdescribes their territory, at the very start, from an organization that has just looked at it.

One thing worth watching: we now ask "isso bate?" after the tour and "o que mais preocupa vocês?" a few beats later. They are different questions — the first is about whether our data describes them, the second about what they actually care about — but if the wording drifts together they will read as the same question asked twice, which is the fastest way to sound like a form.

Sequence: the failsafe list lands regardless and is mostly small. The false tour instruction is already fixed. Next, in order of value per effort: the bairro outline on the tour screens, the pre-selected bairro with its list fallback, the post-tour check, and the inverted-ramp warning. The site step stays where it is — the diagnostic already survives without it.

On caching OSM — already done, and the bottleneck is elsewhere

The question was whether we could hold OSM locally instead of depending on the live service. We already do, and it works: parks, schools, wetlands and hospitals all serve from committed snapshots under knowledge/osm/porto-alegre/, verified returning X-Cache: snapshot in 1–10 ms. That shipped in PR #375, which measured the cold Overpass path at 48.1 s against 8 ms from snapshot. Overpass now exists only as a fallback for a layer with no snapshot.

So the blocking overlay is not waiting on a network service at all. It is waiting on 1.6 MB of JSON — parks 496 KB, schools 374 KB, wetlands 798 KB — being downloaded to the phone and then filtered down to a single bairro in the browser, with hand-rolled point-in-polygon ray-casting. On mobile data that is the whole cost.

The fix is therefore server-side filtering by the bairro's bounds, not more caching. Worth saying plainly because it would be easy to spend a week making the caching better and move nothing.

One related item that is not in E2's path: the per-zone osm-assets query (436 Overpass calls) flagged as open in the snapshot work belongs to the legacy Site Explorer, not the CBO map. E2 is clean on that front.
