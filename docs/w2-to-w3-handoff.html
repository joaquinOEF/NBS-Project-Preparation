What Workshop 2 gives us, and what Workshop 3 should do with it

Workshop 2 is now a diagnostic rather than a recommendation exercise. It gathers what it can in the easiest way possible and establishes where each organization stands — which is what should set how deep Workshop 3 can go. This page sets out exactly what comes out of it, and asks you to check that it is the right handover before we build Workshop 3 around it.

For Vila Flores — Antônia, Aline, Julia For Robson — NBS technical review For Ana — coordination and September clustering

Why we are asking now. Workshop 2 is built and tested. Workshop 3 is not — it exists in the platform only as an empty structure. So this is the moment where changing the handover is cheap. If something below is the wrong thing to collect, or the wrong thing to do with it, telling us this week costs nothing; telling us in October costs a rebuild.

1 · What an organization actually does in Workshop 2

About five minutes of tapping. Everything except naming one worry can be skipped, and skipping is recorded as an answer rather than treated as a failure.

  1. We admit our map is coarse The platform says plainly that each square of its map covers whole blocks, that it cannot see their yard, and that they know the place far better than our data does.
  2. "What worries you most here?" Flooding, heat, the slope, or something else. Multiple choices allowed. The options are ordered by what our data suggests, but the answer is theirs — and when it contradicts our data, that contradiction is recorded as a finding.
  3. They tell us about the place, in their own words A voice note or typed text. Broad prompt, with examples offered rather than a checklist. If they already uploaded a proposal or site description, the platform says so and offers "it's in the file" instead of making them repeat themselves. optional
  4. Photographs — chosen by what worries them If they said water: where it comes in, where it stands, the ground close up. If heat: the space at midday, whatever shade exists, the surface. If the slope: its face, what's above, what's below. Always plus "anything else you think we should see". They can send later. optional
  5. We read our own data back, and they correct it "Our map says flood risk is low in your bairro — but that's an average over the whole neighbourhood, not your place. At your place, is it worse, about the same, or calmer?" A correction outranks our number, visibly: the recommendation that follows says "you told me flooding here is worse than the bairro average."
  6. Five families worth studying — none hidden All five appear, ordered by fit but with nothing removed, and all 27 solutions stay one tap away. Then which families interest them, and what role they would want to play.

If an organization does not have a site yet, none of this is skipped: the whole diagnostic runs at neighbourhood level instead, and the way back to mark a place stays one tap away. Organizations still deciding where to work are exactly the ones we most need to understand, so they should not be the ones we learn least about.

2 · What comes out of it, and what each piece is worth

Being honest about provenance matters more here than volume. Three kinds of thing come out:

they told us the only information measured at the place · our estimate neighbourhood-scale, from satellite and census data · still unknown named openly, because a known gap is useful and a silent one is not

What Workshop 3 could do with it

The place they told us

A marked site, or a neighbourhood when there isn't one yet, plus what the place is like today and whether they can actually use it.

Where scoping starts

W3 opens on a place both sides already agree on, rather than spending its first session establishing one.

What worries them they told us

The hazard they name, which may or may not be the one our data ranks highest.

Which families to go deep on

W3 works through solutions inside the families that match their concern, instead of re-opening the whole catalogue.

Their account of the place they told us

A voice note or text: how the water moves, who uses the space, what is already growing there, how the ground behaves.

The site description a funder asks for

Their own words are the material for the project narrative, and they carry more weight than anything we could infer.

Photographs they told us

Tied to the question each one answers. Ground surface, drainage behaviour, existing vegetation, roof type.

The only view of conditions no satellite has

Whether a roof could take a green roof, whether the ground drains — screening questions with no dataset behind them anywhere.

Their corrections to our data they told us

Whether their place is worse, about the same, or calmer than the neighbourhood figure we quoted them.

A site-level correction to a neighbourhood average

The single cheapest improvement to our numbers, and the one that comes from someone standing in the place.

Where they disagree with us a finding

Recorded whenever what they name differs from what our data ranks — never treated as an error.

The first thing to check in the field

Either our data is wrong at their scale, or something local explains it. Both are worth knowing before designing anything.

What is still unknown still unknown

Named explicitly: land tenure detail, how the ground drains, groundwater depth, what is buried under the street — and anything they answered "I can't say" to.

The mentor's question list, written in advance

W3 opens knowing exactly what it must find out, rather than discovering the gaps halfway through.

Interests and preferred roles they told us

Which families they would want a project in, and whether they see themselves implementing, advising, managing, receiving funds, or connecting partners.

Cluster formation in September

Organizations with overlapping interests, shared risk profiles and complementary roles are the raw material for a portfolio.

A depth read, for the coordination only

How much this organization could tell us, what is missing, and where they disagree with our data. Not a score of the organization, and never shown to them.

Workshop 3 for this organization would open on stormwater solutions at a paved, informally-held public square where water stands two days; would ask first about the buried pipes and the water table, because those two unknowns decide whether the most feasible solutions are possible at all; and would size whatever is chosen against the square's actual area.

3 · What Workshop 2 deliberately does not give us

Worth stating plainly, because the temptation to over-read this data will be strong once it is in front of us.

  • No chosen solution. W2 ends with families worth studying, never one answer. Choosing is Workshop 3's job, with the organization.
  • No sizing and no design. We now hold retention figures per solution type — from Conceito Arte's technical note — but nothing is sized in W2. That needs a defined area and a chosen solution.
  • No feasibility verdict for a specific site. Robson's review gives us feasibility per solution type across the cohort. Whether this organization can build that thing on that land is a Workshop 3 conversation, and partly a mentoring one.
  • No verified tenure. We record what they tell us. Nobody has checked a document.
  • Nothing about groundwater or buried services. No open dataset covers either at city scale — and they are exactly what would rule out rain gardens or a street bioswale, the two solutions rated most feasible. Only local knowledge or a site visit can answer them.

And one thing we now say out loud, from Conceito Arte's own analysis. When an organization describes the 2024 flood, the platform answers honestly: nature-based solutions work very well on everyday water — the rain that floods the street, overloads the drain, the heat that takes over the square. What they do not solve is rivers rising above flood level and dikes failing; that needs macrodrainage works.

It says this only when an organization's own account points at a catastrophe, never as an unprompted lecture about limits. The purpose is to stop anyone designing a project against a problem it cannot solve, and then being disappointed in front of a funder.

4 · What we would like each of you to check

Different questions for different eyes — these are the things we cannot settle ourselves.

Vila Flores

Antônia, Aline, Julia — whether this respects the organizations

  • Does asking for photographs of their own spaces feel like support, or like extraction? The data agreement is not signed yet, and this is the moment to shape it.
  • Is the language right? It is deliberately informal and second-person, and it says openly that our data is poor.
  • Is five minutes of questions before the recommendation too much, or about right?
  • Organizations still deciding where to work now get the full set of questions at neighbourhood level. Does that help them, or does it press them?

Robson

Technical review — whether these are the right questions

  • Are these the right things to ask a community about a site, and what would a technician need that we are not asking?
  • The unknowns we name are tenure, drainage behaviour, groundwater and buried services. Is that the right list, and which of them actually block design rather than merely weakening it?
  • Is the scale framing right to show communities, and at the right moment?
  • When an organization contradicts our hazard data for their own site, should their answer override our number outright — which is what it does today?

Ana

Coordination — whether this serves what comes next

  • Does the depth read give the coordination what it needs to plan September, or is something missing from it?
  • It is coordinator-facing by design, because shown to an organization it would read as a grade. Is that the right call?
  • Are interests and roles enough to start forming clusters, or does that need something we are not capturing?
  • What should happen for an organization whose depth read comes back thin — a conversation before W3, or a different W3?

5 · What happens next

Workshop 2 runs on 12 August. Between now and then we would like the checks above, and we will adjust what we can. Workshop 3 gets designed in September against whatever this handover turns out to be — which is the reason to disagree with it now rather than later.
