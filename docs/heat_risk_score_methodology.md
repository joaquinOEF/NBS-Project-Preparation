# Heat Risk Score Methodology (Porto Alegre)

## 1) Purpose and scope

This document describes how **`heat_risk_score`** is computed for Porto Alegre (POA) in `projects/cougar/heat/script/heat_risk_score.ipynb`.

The product combines:

- **Hazard (H)** — chronic heat hazard ensemble from `heat_hazard_score.ipynb` (documented in `heat_hazard_score_methodology.md`),
- **Exposure (E)** — neighborhood population from **Censo Demográfico 2022** bairro aggregates,
- **Vulnerability (V)** — neighborhood **age-vulnerability share only** (population under 5 or 60+), from the same 2022 sources.

```text
R = (H × E × V)^(1/3)
```

with `f` implemented as a **geometric mean** on a common 0–1 scale.

This is a **screening-level composite index** for prioritization and communication. It is **not** a full IPCC-style risk metric (no explicit coping capacity, no health-outcome calibration, no exposure modeling at the individual or building level).

**Design note (shared E/V):** Exposure and vulnerability are computed **once** in `shared/compute_ev.ipynb` and stored in `shared/output/poa_ev_normalized.gpkg`. All hazard-specific risk notebooks (heat, flood, …) load from this shared file, ensuring consistent normalization bounds and a single source of truth for census data.

**Design note (vulnerability):** V uses **age only** because Censo 2022 bairro aggregates do not publish household poverty at neighborhood level. See §3.4 of the flood risk score methodology for the full explanation.

---

## 2) Conceptual model

| Component | Symbol | Spatial unit | Meaning in this workflow |
|-----------|--------|--------------|--------------------------|
| Hazard | `H` | Raster (250 m) | Relative chronic heat intensity (ensemble of Landsat 8 + MODIS Day + MODIS Night P90 LST) |
| Exposure | `E` | Neighborhood → rasterized | Relative population presence (proxy for people in harm's way) |
| Vulnerability | `V` | Neighborhood → rasterized | Relative age-related social susceptibility (share of population 0–4 or 60+) |
| Risk | `R` | Raster (250 m) | Combined screening score where H, E, and V are all defined |

**Key design choice:** E and V are **administrative neighborhood attributes** (constant within each bairro polygon), while H varies **within** the bairro based on the thermal remote-sensing ensemble. Risk therefore varies spatially mainly through H, with step changes at bairro borders for E and V.

**Difference from flood risk:** Unlike the flood hazard, which is typically concentrated along river corridors, the heat hazard covers the **full urban area** of Porto Alegre. This means heat risk pixels exist across the entire city, not just in flood-prone zones, leading to broader spatial coverage in the risk raster.

## 3) Input datasets

### 3.1 Hazard (upstream product)

| Item | Path / source |

`H` is a **250 m ensemble** of three normalized thermal layers, combined as an arithmetic mean:

```text
H = mean(Landsat 8 P90 LST norm, MODIS Day P90 LST norm, MODIS Night P90 LST norm)
```

All layers cover DJF 2015–2024 (austral summer). ERA5-Land was considered and excluded due to insufficient spatial resolution over POA (~3×3 pixels); see `heat_hazard_score_methodology.md` §4.

## 4) Normalization (neighborhood level)

Normalization is **min–max within the POA neighborhood sample** computed once in `shared/compute_ev.ipynb`:

```text
x_norm = (x - min_POA) / (max_POA - min_POA)
```

### 4.1 Exposure (`exposure_score`)

```text
population_density = total_population / area_km²   (SIRGAS 2000 / UTM 22S polygon area)
exposure_score     = minmax_norm(population_density)
```

**Population density is used instead of total population** to avoid a systematic size bias: larger bairros tend to have more absolute inhabitants simply because they cover more area, not because they are more densely inhabited. Density better reflects the number of people present per unit of hazard area — which is what matters when each 250 m heat hazard pixel is paired with a bairro exposure value.

For heat risk specifically, this is especially important: since the heat hazard covers the full urban area, every bairro contributes to R across all its pixels. Using density ensures that a small, densely populated downtown bairro is not penalized relative to a large peripheral one simply due to geometry.

Higher score = higher population density relative to other POA neighborhoods.

**Known limitation — area denominator:** The polygon area includes **all land use types**: parks, water bodies, industrial zones, and uninhabited green belts. This can underestimate real residential density in bairros with large non-residential areas (e.g., a bairro with a major park will appear less dense than its residential fabric would suggest).

**Planned improvement:** Replace total polygon area with **built-up or residential footprint area** (e.g., Global Human Settlement Layer, OpenStreetMap building footprints, or IBGE urban area polygons) to obtain a net residential density that excludes non-inhabited land from the denominator.

### 4.2 Vulnerability (`vulnerability_score`)

```text
vulnerability_score = minmax_norm(vulnerable_percentage)
```

where `vulnerable_percentage` is the share of residents aged 0–4 or 60+.

**Single normalization run:** Because E/V are computed once in `shared/compute_ev.ipynb`, the min and max anchors are stable across all hazard products. Re-running that notebook would shift normalization bounds and invalidate previously published rasters.

## 5) Joining attributes to polygons and rasterization

### 5.1 Join

`location_id` (= `CD_BAIRRO` as string) links the canonical GeoPackage attributes to the IBGE 2022 boundary polygons. After the join, only bairros with **both** `exposure_score` and `vulnerability_score` non-null are used for rasterization.

### 5.2 Rasterization onto the heat hazard grid

E and V are burned onto the **250 m heat hazard grid** (same CRS, transform, width, height as `heat_hazard_score_poa.tif`) using `burn_to_grid()` from `shared/risk_utils.py`:

- `rasterio.features.rasterize` with `all_touched=True`
- Polygons sorted by **ascending area**; larger bairros overwrite smaller at borders
- Output: `float32` arrays in memory — **not saved to disk** (canonical rasters live in `shared/output/`)

E and V are **constant within** each bairro; sub-neighborhood variation in R comes entirely from H.

### 5.3 Coverage interaction with H

| Layer | Spatial extent |
|-------|----------------|
| `exposure_grid`, `vulnerability_grid` | All cells overlapping bairro polygons (full city) |
| `hazard` (H) | All cells with valid thermal data (full city, minus persistent cloud/water gaps) |
| `risk_grid` (R) | **Intersection:** finite H **and** finite E **and** finite V |

Because the heat hazard covers the full urban area, risk coverage is broader than in the flood case (where H is concentrated along river corridors).

---

## 6) Risk score formula

```text
R = clip( (H × E × V)^(1/3), 0, 1 )
```

Computed only where `isfinite(H) & isfinite(E) & isfinite(V)`.

The **geometric mean**:
- keeps H, E, V on comparable 0–1 footing,
- prevents any single component from dominating by magnitude alone,
- yields 0 if any component is 0 (e.g., a bairro with no population has zero risk regardless of H).

This is an index construction choice, not a calibrated heat-health damage function.

---

## 7) Bairro-level aggregation

For each bairro polygon, the notebook computes the **mean of `risk_grid` pixels** falling within the polygon (via `geometry_mask` from `rasterio.features`). This mean is saved as `heat_risk_score` in the output GeoPackage.

The summary table also carries:
- `heat_hazard_mean` — mean pixel-level H within the bairro
- `heat_exposure_score` — bairro-level E (from canonical GeoPackage)
- `heat_vulnerability_score` — bairro-level V (from canonical GeoPackage)

## 9) Assumptions

1. **Censo 2022 bairro aggregates** represent current neighborhood structure and are comparable across bairros after min–max scaling.
2. **Population is an adequate exposure proxy** for screening (no building value, outdoor worker count, or daily mobility considered).
3. **Vulnerable age share (0–4 and 60+)** is an acceptable single-dimension vulnerability proxy (no poverty, health comorbidities, housing quality, or air conditioning access in current V).
4. **Geometric mean** is an acceptable combiner for H, E, V on [0, 1] scales.
5. **Heat hazard validity** is inherited from the upstream thermal ensemble methodology (see `heat_hazard_score_methodology.md`).
6. **Neighborhood-constant E/V** is acceptable for stakeholder communication when documented; intra-bairro risk variation comes entirely from the 250 m thermal data.
7. **Land Surface Temperature as heat hazard proxy:** LST measures the radiometric surface skin temperature, not the 2-m air temperature experienced by people. High LST in paved areas may not translate proportionally to pedestrian heat exposure.
8. **Summer-only (DJF) period:** Spring or early-autumn heat events are not captured in H.

---

## 10) Key limitations

1. **Not full IPCC risk:** no adaptive capacity term; V is a simplified age-only proxy.
2. **Poverty excluded from V:** not available at bairro level in Censo 2022 aggregates.
3. **Area denominator in E:** Population density uses total polygon area, which includes parks, water bodies, and industrial zones. Bairros with large non-residential areas (e.g., Parque Marinha do Brasil, Jardim Botânico) may have underestimated density scores. Planned improvement: use built-up or residential footprint area as denominator.
4. **LST ≠ air temperature:** surface-derived H is a structural proxy, not a measured heat exposure metric. Outdoor thermal comfort requires wet-bulb temperature, shade fraction, and wind, which are not modeled here.
4. **No heat-health calibration:** the score is not anchored to excess mortality, hospitalization, or other health outcomes for Porto Alegre.
5. **Cloud cover gaps in H:** optical/thermal sensors cannot observe through clouds. Persistent DJF cloud cover in some areas reduces the number of valid Landsat/MODIS observations entering the P90 composite, potentially underestimating heat in those pixels.
6. **No urban morphology in V:** building density, green cover, and air conditioning access are known moderators of heat vulnerability but are not included in the current V component.
7. **Bairro data gaps:** up to 5 boundary polygons lack census rows and are excluded from rasterization.
8. **No uncertainty propagation:** H, E, and V are point estimates; no confidence bands on R.

## 11) Relationship to flood risk score

Both heat and flood risk products use:
- the **same E and V** (identical normalized values, from `shared/compute_ev.ipynb`)
- the **same geometric mean formula** `R = (H × E × V)^(1/3)`
- the **same bairro boundaries** (IBGE 2022)
- the **same shared utilities** (`shared/risk_utils.py`)

They differ only in:
- the **hazard input** (thermal ensemble vs. fluvial flood ensemble)
- the **spatial extent of H** (full city for heat; river-corridor concentrated for flood)
- the **underlying physical process** being screened

This shared framework allows direct comparison of bairro-level heat and flood risk scores on the same 0–1 scale.

## 12) Recommended use

**Appropriate uses:**
- neighborhood prioritization for heat adaptation interventions,
- identifying bairros where high thermal hazard coincides with large or age-vulnerable populations,
- internal dashboards combining heat and flood risk on a common scale,
- NBS / green infrastructure screening when framed as index-based triage.

**Not appropriate alone for:**
- heat-health burden estimation (requires epidemiological models),
- urban heat island mitigation design (requires higher-resolution microclimate modeling),
- regulatory heat-risk assessment without independent validation,
- claims of validated probabilistic risk without health-outcome calibration.

## 14) Workflow summary (implementation order)

1. Run `heat/script/lst_landsat8.ipynb` → `lst_lc08_norm_djf_2015_2024_poa.tif`
2. Run `heat/script/MOD11A2.ipynb` → `mod11a2_lst_day_norm_*.tif`, `mod11a2_lst_night_norm_*.tif`
3. Run `heat/script/heat_hazard_score.ipynb` → `heat_hazard_score_poa.tif`
4. Run `shared/compute_ev.ipynb` *(once, or when CSVs/boundaries change)*:
   - Computes polygon area (km²)
   - Computes population density = total_population / area_km²
   - Normalizes E (density) and V (age %) across POA
   - Saves `poa_ev_normalized.gpkg`, canonical rasters, and tiles
5. Run `heat/script/heat_risk_score.ipynb` → `heat_risk_score_poa.tif`, `heat_risk_score_poa.gpkg`, COG + tiles
