# Landslide Risk Score Methodology (Porto Alegre)

## 1) Purpose and scope

This document describes how **`landslide_risk_score`** is computed for Porto Alegre (POA) in `projects/cougar/landslides/script/landslide_risk_score.ipynb`.

The product combines:

- **Hazard (H)** — landslide susceptibility score from `landslide_hazard_score.ipynb` (documented in `landslide_hazard_score_methodology.md`),
- **Exposure (E)** — neighborhood population density from **Censo Demográfico 2022** bairro aggregates,
- **Vulnerability (V)** — neighborhood **age-vulnerability share** (population under 5 or 60+), from the same 2022 sources.

```text
R = (H × E × V)^(1/3)
```

implemented as a **geometric mean** on a common 0–1 scale.

This is a **screening-level composite susceptibility index** for prioritization and communication — not a calibrated probabilistic risk estimate (no failure return periods, no exposure at the individual or building level, no modeled casualty counts).

**Design note (shared E/V):** Exposure and vulnerability are computed **once** in `shared/compute_ev.ipynb` and stored in `shared/output/poa_ev_normalized.gpkg`. All hazard-specific risk notebooks (heat, flood, landslide) load from this shared file, ensuring consistent normalization and a single source of truth for census data.

**Design note (resolution):** The landslide hazard operates at **90 m** — finer than the 250 m grid used for flood and heat — to preserve slope detail critical for landslide initiation. E and V (stored as bairro polygon attributes) are burned onto the 90 m hazard grid for this product specifically. The shared E/V data itself is unchanged; only the rasterization target differs.

---

## 2) Conceptual model

| Component | Symbol | Spatial unit | Meaning in this workflow |
|-----------|--------|--------------|--------------------------|
| Hazard | `H` | Raster (**90 m**) | Relative landslide susceptibility (terrain, climate, soil, vegetation, drainage ensemble) |
| Exposure | `E` | Neighborhood → rasterized at 90 m | Relative population density (people per km² relative to other POA bairros) |
| Vulnerability | `V` | Neighborhood → rasterized at 90 m | Relative age-related social susceptibility (share of population 0–4 or 60+) |
| Risk | `R` | Raster (**90 m**) | Combined screening score where H, E, and V are all defined |

**Key design choice:** E and V are **administrative neighborhood attributes** (constant within each bairro polygon), while H varies **within** the bairro based on terrain and climate. Risk therefore varies spatially mainly through H, with step changes at bairro borders for E and V.

**Key difference from flood and heat risk:** Landslide hazard is concentrated on **steeper hillslopes** (slope ≥ 15°). In the flat, low-lying areas that characterize Porto Alegre's lowlands and river valleys, `slope_risk = 0` by design, producing `H = 0` and therefore `R = 0`. Risk coverage is narrower than flood or heat risk but at higher spatial resolution.

## 3) Input datasets

### 3.1 Hazard (upstream product)

| Item | Path / source |
|------|----------------|
| Operational raster | `projects/cougar/landslides/output_data/landslide_hazard_score_poa_90m.tif` |

| Notebook | `projects/cougar/landslides/script/landslide_hazard_score.ipynb` |

`H` is a **90 m weighted ensemble** of five terrain and climate components:

```text
H = clamp01(
    0.45 × slope_risk
  + 0.20 × precip_risk  × slope_risk
  + 0.15 × low_cohesion × slope_risk
  + 0.10 × lack_of_veg  × slope_risk
  + 0.10 × hand_factor  × slope_risk
)
± land cover modifier (Dynamic World)
```

Flat areas (slope < 15°) score `H = 0` regardless of other inputs. See `landslide_hazard_score_methodology.md` for full layer descriptions, weight rationale, and formula derivation.

## 4) Normalization (neighborhood level)

Normalization is **min–max within the POA neighborhood sample**, computed once in `shared/compute_ev.ipynb`:

```text
x_norm = (x - min_POA) / (max_POA - min_POA)
```

### 4.1 Exposure (`exposure_score`)

```text
population_density = total_population / area_km²   (SIRGAS 2000 / UTM 22S polygon area)
exposure_score     = minmax_norm(population_density)
```

**Population density is used instead of total population** to avoid a systematic size bias: larger bairros tend to have more absolute inhabitants simply because they cover more area. Density better reflects the number of people present per unit of hazard area.

For landslide risk specifically, this matters because the hazard is concentrated on steep hillslopes that often correspond to peripheral bairros with low total populations but moderate densities on the inhabited slopes. Using density prevents penalizing these bairros relative to flat, populous downtown areas.

**Known limitation — area denominator:** The polygon area includes all land use types: parks, water bodies, industrial zones. Bairros with large non-residential areas may appear less dense than their residential fabric suggests.

**Planned improvement:** Replace total polygon area with built-up or residential footprint area (GHSL, OSM building footprints, or IBGE urban area polygons) to obtain net residential density.

### 4.2 Vulnerability (`vulnerability_score`)

```text
vulnerability_score = minmax_norm(vulnerable_percentage)
```

where `vulnerable_percentage` is the share of residents aged 0–4 or 60+.

**Age is used as the sole vulnerability dimension** because Censo 2022 bairro aggregates do not publish household poverty or housing quality at neighborhood level. For landslide risk, additional relevant dimensions not currently captured include: housing construction type (informal settlements on slopes are at higher physical risk), access to early warning systems, and proximity to emergency services.

## 5) Rasterization onto the 90 m hazard grid

E and V are burned onto the **90 m landslide hazard grid** (same CRS, transform, width, height as `landslide_hazard_score_poa_90m.tif`) using `burn_to_grid()` from `shared/risk_utils.py`:

- `rasterio.features.rasterize` with `all_touched=True`
- Polygons sorted by **ascending area**; larger bairros overwrite smaller at borders
- Output: `float32` arrays in memory — **not saved to disk** (canonical rasters live in `shared/output/`)

**Resolution note:** `burn_to_grid()` is resolution-agnostic. For flood and heat risk notebooks it uses the 250 m hazard meta; here it uses the 90 m landslide hazard meta. The same bairro polygon data is used in all cases — only the rasterization target changes.

E and V are **constant within** each bairro; sub-neighborhood variation in R comes entirely from H.

### Coverage interaction

| Layer | Spatial extent |
|-------|----------------|
| `exposure_grid`, `vulnerability_grid` | All 90 m cells overlapping bairro polygons (full city) |
| `hazard` (H) | Cells with valid slope data; zero where slope < 15° |
| `risk_grid` (R) | **Intersection:** finite H **and** finite E **and** finite V |

Because H is zero (not NaN) on flat terrain, R is computed everywhere bairro polygons have E/V coverage, but equals zero on flat land. Non-zero R only appears on slopes ≥ 15° within inhabited bairros.

---

## 6) Risk score formula

```text
R = clip( (H × E × V)^(1/3), 0, 1 )
```

Computed only where `isfinite(H) & isfinite(E) & isfinite(V)`.

The **geometric mean**:
- keeps H, E, V on comparable 0–1 footing,
- prevents any single component from dominating by magnitude alone,
- yields R = 0 if any component is 0 (a bairro with no population, or a flat area, produces zero risk regardless of the other inputs).

This is an index construction choice, not a calibrated landslide damage or casualty function.

---

## 7) Bairro-level aggregation

For each bairro polygon, the notebook computes the **mean of `risk_grid` pixels** falling within the polygon (via `geometry_mask` from `rasterio.features`). This mean is saved as `landslide_risk_score` in the output GeoPackage.

The summary table also carries:
- `landslide_hazard_mean` — mean pixel-level H within the bairro
- `landslide_exposure_score` — bairro-level E (from canonical GeoPackage)
- `landslide_vulnerability_score` — bairro-level V (from canonical GeoPackage)

Bairros dominated by flat terrain (e.g., downtown, riverfront) will have low mean H and consequently low mean R even if E and V are high.

## 9) Assumptions

1. **Censo 2022 bairro aggregates** represent current neighborhood structure and are comparable across bairros after min–max scaling.
2. **Population density is an adequate exposure proxy** for screening (no building value, outdoor worker count, or daily mobility considered).
3. **Vulnerable age share (0–4 and 60+)** is an acceptable single-dimension vulnerability proxy (no informal housing quality, proximity to slope, access to early warning, or social capital in current V).
4. **Geometric mean** is an acceptable combiner for H, E, V on [0, 1] scales.
5. **Landslide hazard validity** is inherited from the upstream susceptibility methodology (see `landslide_hazard_score_methodology.md`).
6. **Neighborhood-constant E/V** is acceptable for stakeholder communication when documented; intra-bairro risk variation comes entirely from the 90 m terrain data.
7. **Static susceptibility:** the hazard score reflects terrain and climate conditions at the time of data acquisition; it does not capture time-varying factors such as antecedent soil moisture or seasonal vegetation dynamics.

---

## 10) Key limitations

1. **Susceptibility ≠ hazard frequency:** H captures predisposing conditions, not the annual probability of a landslide event. A high H pixel may never fail in the observation period; a lower-H pixel may fail under an extreme precipitation event not captured by the R90p climatology.
2. **Not full IPCC risk:** no adaptive capacity term; V is a simplified age-only proxy without housing quality or informal settlement dimension.
3. **Area denominator in E:** Population density uses total polygon area, which includes parks, water bodies, and industrial zones. Peripheral bairros with large protected areas (e.g., forests, reservoirs) may have underestimated density scores.
4. **Slope resolution:** Even at 90 m, individual narrow ravines or cut slopes (< 30 m wide) may be averaged below the 15° activation threshold. Field-scale hazard assessment on specific sites requires LiDAR or higher-resolution DEMs.
5. **No temporal dynamics:** a single R90p climatology snapshot is used. Future changes in extreme precipitation under climate change are not propagated into H.
6. **MERIT HAND tile gaps:** NoData pixels in MERIT Hydro (water bodies, some tile edges) are filled with a neutral default (25 m). This may over- or under-estimate drainage contribution near rivers and coastline.
7. **No validation against inventory:** the score has not been validated against the CEMADEN or INMET historical landslide inventory for Porto Alegre. Calibration of the 15°/35° thresholds and weights to local failure data is a recommended next step.
8. **No uncertainty propagation:** H, E, and V are point estimates; no confidence intervals on R.
9. **Bairro data gaps:** boundary polygons lacking census rows are excluded from rasterization.

## 11) Relationship to flood and heat risk scores

All three risk products (flood, heat, landslide) share:
- the **same E and V** (identical normalized values from `shared/compute_ev.ipynb`)
- the **same geometric mean formula** `R = (H × E × V)^(1/3)`
- the **same bairro boundaries** (IBGE 2022)
- the **same shared utilities** (`shared/risk_utils.py`)

They differ in:

| Aspect | Flood | Heat | Landslide |
|--------|-------|------|-----------|
| Hazard grid resolution | 250 m | 250 m | **90 m** |
| E/V rasterization resolution | 250 m | 250 m | **90 m** |
| H spatial extent | River corridors | Full city | Steeper hillslopes (slope ≥ 15°) |
| H data sources | Flood inundation model | Landsat + MODIS LST | DEM slope, CHIRPS, SoilGrids, MODIS NDVI, MERIT HAND |
| Physical process | Fluvial inundation | Chronic thermal stress | Shallow landslide initiation |

This shared framework allows **direct comparison** of bairro-level risk scores across all three hazards on the same 0–1 scale, provided users understand the different physical processes and resolution assumptions.

## 12) Recommended use

**Appropriate uses:**
- neighborhood prioritization for landslide risk reduction and NBS interventions on slopes,
- identifying bairros where steep terrain coincides with dense, age-vulnerable populations,
- combined multi-hazard dashboards with flood and heat risk on a common scale,
- green infrastructure screening (e.g., reforestation, bioengineering) using the hazard layer.

**Not appropriate alone for:**
- site-specific slope stability assessment (requires geotechnical investigation),
- landslide early warning (requires real-time rainfall thresholds and soil moisture),
- regulatory land-use decision-making without site-specific validation,
- casualty or damage estimation without exposure models at building or household level.

## 15) Planned improvements

| Component | Current approach | Planned improvement |
|-----------|-----------------|---------------------|
| Exposure (E) | Population density using total polygon area | Replace denominator with built-up / residential footprint (GHSL, OSM buildings) to exclude parks and non-inhabited land |
| Vulnerability (V) | Age-only (0–4 and 60+) | Add landslide-specific dimensions: informal housing on slopes (census dwelling type), distance from escape routes, access to early warning systems |
| Hazard (H) | Static susceptibility from decadal climate data | Incorporate dynamic antecedent rainfall (e.g., cumulative 5-day rainfall from CHIRPS) as a time-varying trigger layer; validate against CEMADEN inventory |
| Resolution | 90 m canonical grid | Higher resolution (e.g., 30 m) feasible when E/V move from bairro polygons to gridded population products (WorldPop, GHSL) |
