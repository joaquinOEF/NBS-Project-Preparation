NBS Project Builder · COUGAR · Workshop 2 · design alternatives

What a 250-metre pixel cannot know about a community garden

The W2 recommendation ranks five NBS families from data that was never measured at the place an organization chose — and in several cases was never measured anywhere near it. This sets out honestly what our data can and cannot say at site scale, then puts five alternatives on the table for letting the organizations close the gap themselves.

What W2 is for (decided 31 July): not a precise recommendation — a diagnostic. Gather as much as we can in the easiest possible way, and establish where each organization stands, which is what determines how deep Workshop 3 can go. So uncertainty widens the aperture here and narrowing is W3's job. What an organization can tell us is itself the signal, not just the input.

Sections 1–3 are the evidence: what our data can and cannot say, and what the platform already receives. Section 4 is the option space. Section 5 is the decided model — read that first if you only read one.

1 · The asymmetry

Porto Alegre's ~497 km² is divided into 94 usable IBGE census bairros — a mean bairro of about 500 hectares, and Arquipélago alone is 6,600. A community organization's site is a square, a school yard, a vacant lot: 0.3–1 hectare.

Here is what that means for the numbers we show them. Each layer's native cell, against a 0.3 ha praça:

A 55 × 55 m yard takes its flood and heat numbers from a cell twenty-one times its size, and its rainfall trigger from a cell covering roughly a fifth of the municipality. ERA5-Land resolves to just 3 × 3 pixels over the whole metro area — which is exactly why it was excluded from the operational heat ensemble, though eight ERA5-derived layers and two "schools in heatwave zones" query cards still ship.

It is worse than resolution, in three specific ways

Two of the three factors in every risk score are bairro constants. Risk is (H × E × V)^⅓. Hazard varies at 250 m; exposure (population density) and vulnerability (share aged 0–4 and 60+) are identical for every site in the bairro — stated verbatim in all three methodology docs. For a 0.3 ha site those are averages over an area 1,600× to 22,000× larger. And exposure's denominator is total bairro area including parks, water and industry, so a bairro with a big park reads as less dense than the streets people actually live on.

The site's shape is discarded. When an organization draws a polygon, the app reduces it to geometryCentroid() — an unweighted mean of the vertices, not an area centroid — and samples one pixel there (MapMicroapp.tsx:1304-1317). No zonal statistic is ever computed. An L-shaped lot gets located toward whichever arm has more vertices.

And the ranking doesn't even use that point. The recommendation reads _bairro_flood_pct — the neighbourhood figure (cboAgent.ts:1332, written at :1494) — while the site's own lat/lng sits unused beside it. Two organizations in the same bairro, one on a hilltop and one in a hollow, get identical hazard inputs and therefore the same family order.

The published product is deliberately the filled raster. The methodology is candid about what that means: filled gaps "have no local hazard evidence; they inherit context from observed neighbours", and interpolation "can smooth transitions and produce mixed in bands where direct screening would be more binary". Interpolated values are "inferred screening scores, not new observations", and the doc asks that they "be flagged (is_interpolated) and not conflated with direct ensemble outputs".

The flag exists as a raster product. It is surfaced nowhere. Searching the entire client, server and shared code for is_interpolated returns three hits: a Leaflet attribution string, an i18n config line, and a colour-ramp function. On hover, a gap-filled pixel and a directly-screened one both read "Mixed", identically. The tooltip prints the value to three decimal places either way.

Alongside that, a long list of values are substituted defaults no one is told about: missing HAND becomes 0.3; missing rainfall trigger, soil cohesion and permeability all become 0.5 (SoilGrids covers 64% of the city); missing elevation becomes the city's 25th percentile; ~46% of grid cells get their bairro by nearest-centroid fallback rather than point-in-polygon. And when a layer has no data at the site at all, its line is simply omitted from the tooltip — an absence, never a "we can't say here".

Some classes we would rank on can never appear

This one bears directly on the mechanism-informed ranking proposed in the companion doc. Of the flood mechanism classes, drainage_constrained never occurs anywhere in Porto Alegre — it is a proxy awaiting municipal drainage data we do not have. Of the ten landslide mechanism classes, four never appear, and most of the city is nodata because the layer is gated to hazard > 0. Heat lacks limited_nocturnal_cooling entirely. A recommendation rule keyed to a phantom class is dead code that looks like intelligence.

Two further cautions on the same layer: riverine=True and flood_mechanism_type=none can legitimately coexist (different thresholds), and the landslide ramp's 98th percentile across POA is only 0.393, so the entire city renders green and real variation is invisible.

The provenance we do show is out of date

data-provenance.ts is the only "about this data" surface in the product, and it currently tells users the flood layer is built from the May 2024 Copernicus emergency mapping — while the shipped hazard ensemble is JRC GloFAS RP100 (0.45), the Global Flood Database (0.30), WRI Aqueduct v2 (0.15) and GFPLAIN250m (0.10). It credits ERA5-Land for heat, which was explicitly excluded. Separately, the flood model's own validation is compromised by leakage: the May 2024 depth raster is a 15%-weight input and the validation label, and the audit's gate table answers "F1 = 70% validated model?" with a flat No.

They already know their territory. What the platform adds is a source they can put in front of a funder.

Ana's framing, PxG<>OEF biweekly, 16 July — the reason to formalise local knowledge rather than replace it

Which is the uncomfortable inversion at the centre of this: we are asking communities to trust our numbers as the formal evidence for their projects, when at their scale their own knowledge is the better instrument. In a REDD+ study, participatory mapping added 39 land-cover classes to the 8 a remote-sensing desk assessment produced. At site scale, community knowledge isn't a garnish on the raster — it is most of the map.

Read the third column downward: not one input to the family ranking is measured at the site. Two are neighbourhood averages, three are interpolated or proxied, five don't exist. The organization sitting in front of the screen is the highest-resolution instrument in the system — and on the two questions that would actually rule out Robson's most-feasible solutions (groundwater, buried pipes) they are the only instrument.

Most of what we need is already built, then dropped at a parse boundary

This reframes the whole exercise. Several of the alternatives below are not "build intake" but "stop discarding what arrives":

  • Point-level raster values are already sampled on every map click and serialised into the confirm message — and the server's two regexes capture only zone names, three percentages, and lat/lng. The site-level numbers travel the whole way and are dropped in the parse.
  • The offline zone data is far richer than what survives: a full H×E×V decomposition, flood-extent percentage, vulnerability factor, population and priority score are computed, rendered in tooltips — then collapsed to three integers (3 decimals → toFixed(0) → parseInt) before anything stores them.
  • Uploads already get heavy AI extraction — vision OCR on every image with a verbatim-transcription prompt, PDF page vision, PPTX, DOCX, XLSX, and audio transcription — and reach the agent four ways, including a DOCUMENTS ON FILE block in every system prompt. The 8 July "photo analysis is experimental" caveat is about trusting it, not about building it.
  • But photo EXIF/GPS is discarded — no code path reads it. A photo taken at the site carries its own coordinates and we throw them away.
  • Voice input already works — recorder → transcription endpoint → normal text turn. The audio isn't retained, but the capture exists.
  • Polygon drawing is implemented and hidden — the E2 focused-site map runs in simpleSite mode, which removes the draw controls; geometry that does get through is stored in cohort_members.site where nothing reads it.
  • The community-anchoring composer is fully built and unreachable, along with priority ranking — both explicitly deferred in the E2 skill.

One provenance defect to fix before alternative E is honest. The support inbox links coordinators to the CBO's own profile page, which is unauthenticated and has no principal distinction — so a coordinator gets full edit rights over every field, and handleCboEdit hard-codes source: 'user'. A coordinator's inference is recorded as the organization's own statement, and the edit re-invokes the agent, which can cascade into further writes and append turns to the org's transcript.

Related, and relevant to B: the upload routes have no auth or cohort check, and GET /api/documents/:id/original is completely ungated — the raw file is the least protected artefact in the chain. That needs closing before we invite a cohort to photograph their sites, and certainly before the data-governance agreement is signed.

The precondition: stop showing inferred values as measured

None of the alternatives below are honest while the platform still presents a gap-filled 250 m average as a fact about someone's yard. Four cheap moves, none of which need a single new answer from anyone:

  • Surface is_interpolated — the raster already exists; the tooltip and the site card should distinguish "screened here" from "inferred from neighbours within 750 m".
  • Say the cell size next to the site, once, in words they can act on: "one square of this map covers about six hectares — several blocks."
  • Render nodata as a statement — "we can't say here" — instead of an omitted line.
  • Fix data-provenance.ts so the sources it names are the sources actually in the ensemble.

There is a precedent to extend rather than invent: estimated: true on NBS catalogue content, rendered as a visible mark with "estimated classification, under verification". It is the only per-value honesty flag in the product — and today it covers the solution cards, not a single geospatial number.

6 · The aperture rule

Because W2 is a diagnostic rather than a verdict, the ranking has to behave differently according to how much it actually knows — otherwise we are just more confident about the same thin data. Three states, visible to the organization:

one or two answers confirmedSignals gain a "you told us" mark; examples re-picked; order may shift"Because you told me the water sits two days, these moved up."
they answered "I'm not sure"Widens rather than guesses; keeps both branches alive and flags it for the coordinator"No problem — I'll keep both possibilities open and the coordination can look at it with you."

And the guarantee that makes any of this non-prescriptive, worth stating in the interface: nothing an organization answers ever removes a solution from the catalogue. Answers change order, examples and emphasis. All 27 stay reachable with Robson's honest feasibility badges, one tap away — which is also the safety net for when our data is simply wrong, which §1 establishes it sometimes is.

Where the alternatives landed. D and B become beats 2 and 3, in that order, because the organization speaking first is both the better diagnostic and the less extractive posture. A becomes beat 4 — a read-back of their words before our pixel, rather than the opening move. C is not in W2: the map is already the flow's hardest step, and EXIF from beat 3 gets us site-level location for free. E moves to W3, where the depth read tells the coordination who needs it.

Still open

  1. Photos are asynchronous — does the read-back wait? Proposal: read back in-session on the story alone; photos arriving later enrich the depth read and W3 rather than re-opening W2. Alternative: a short second read-back when photos land.
  2. Does a confirmed answer override the pixel outright, or re-weight it? Overriding is what "their knowledge outranks our raster" means — but it makes one tap heavier than an entire hazard model.
  3. Do we parse the point samples we already send? Site-level raster values ride the confirm message today and are dropped by the server's regex. Nearly free to read — though it only fixes hazard; exposure and vulnerability stay bairro constants regardless.
  4. Route auth before inviting photos. Separate from the attribution question and, I'd argue, not optional: uploads have no auth and the original-file route is ungated.
  5. Phantom mechanism classes — build affinities only on classes that actually occur in POA, and decide what the agent says when the class is a gap-filled "mixed".
  6. Tenure — it scores site_control today but touches neither the ranking nor feasibility framing. Wire it into W3 scoping, or leave it as a maturity signal only?
  7. How much of the honesty is user-facing — the cell-size line is now beat 0, but interpolation flags and nodata-as-a-statement could stay coordinator-side.
